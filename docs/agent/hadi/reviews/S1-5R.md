# S1-5R review: C6b (display, bulb) and C7 (budget, persistence)

Reviewer: review-5 (did not write the work, wrote no code). Date 2026-11-14.
Reviewed read-only from the main checkout: `proposals/C6b_display_bulb.md`, `proposals/C7_budget.md`. Cross-read: C1, C3, C4, C5, C6a, and `reviews/S1-3R.md`.
Primary sources I actually opened and text-extracted: DS39881E (PIC24FJ64GA004 family), the WS2812B (old) datasheet, the WS2812B-V5 datasheet, DS70157 (dsPIC30F/33F Programmer's Reference Manual, same ISA), the XC16 v2.10 linker script `p24FJ64GA002.gld`, and the FastLED `chipsets.h` header. I also compiled and linked test files with xc16-gcc v2.10 (scratch dir, not in the repo).
C3 energy scaling (shift 6 to 8 vs isqrt) and the C4/C5 fixes (offset-fold sign, mshift range, kNN precedence) are **in flux**. They are noted below, not reconciled to exact numbers.

## Verdict: REQUEST CHANGES (doc fixes only; the architecture is sound)

The big picture holds. Bit-bang with chunked masking is a reasonable choice. IR NEC primary with a WS2812 fallback is sound for a student demo. RAM and flash are far from binding. But three things must be fixed before Hadi decides:
1. C6b proposes pins that collide with C6a's map (RB12, RB13, RB14).
2. C7's flash persistence design does not fit: an A/B copy of the 1.2 KB template blob cannot live in one 1024 B erase block when only 16 of each 24 bits per word are used.
3. C7's RAM table omits C1's pre-trigger ring and drain staging, and assumes the FIFO is read "straight into the capture buffer", which C1's design forbids.

Smaller issues: the WS2812 bit timings sit at the edge of the V5 window; the UART overflow window is computed from the wrong worst case; the "no overlap" claim is right but the compromise timing can be chosen much better.

## 1. WS2812 timing: arithmetic

Datasheet values, both extracted from the PDFs (see fact-check table):
- Old: T0H 0.40 +/-0.15 = [0.25, 0.55]. T0L 0.85 +/-0.15 = [0.70, 1.00]. T1H 0.80 +/-0.15 = [0.65, 0.95]. T1L 0.45 +/-0.15 = [0.30, 0.60]. RES above 50 us. VIH 0.7 VDD.
- V5: T0H [0.22, 0.38]. T0L, T1H, T1L each [0.58, 1.00]. RES above 280 us. VIH 0.63 VDD.

Intersection of the two windows, then 62.5 ns steps (n cycles = n x 0.0625 us):

| Symbol | Intersection | Cycle counts that fit | Result |
|--------|--------------|-----------------------|--------|
| T0H | [0.25, 0.38] | 4 (0.250, edge), **5 (0.3125, centered)**, 6 (0.375, within 5 ns of V5 max) | fits |
| T0L | [0.70, 1.00] | 12 (0.75) to 16 (1.00) | fits |
| T1H | [0.65, 0.95] | 11 (0.6875) to 15 (0.9375); 13 (0.8125) is the center | fits |
| T1L | [0.58, 0.60] | 9 = 0.5625 (below 0.58), 10 = 0.625 (above 0.60). **No integer multiple of 62.5 ns lies in [0.58, 0.60]** | **empty** |

So the claim "no single cycle-counted waveform satisfies both" is **CONFIRMED**, and it fails only on T1L. The failure is small: 9 cycles misses V5 by 17.5 ns, 10 cycles misses the old part by 25 ns.

C6b's choice (T1L = 7 cycles = 0.4375 us) misses V5 by 142 ns, which is needlessly large. Suggested fix: T0 = 5H/14L, T1 = 13H/9L. The per-bit period is free (the datasheets allow 1.25 us +/-600 ns), so 1-bits may be 22 cycles. That puts every edge within 18 ns of every window. It costs 2 extra cycles per 1-bit. Make T1L a compile-time `WS2812_T1L_CYCLES` (9 for old, 10 for V5) rather than one compromise. The bit-bang still needs a scope check; the 20-cycle feasibility is by cycle count only (taken BRA is 2 cycles, DS70157 table "1 (2)").

Practice (informational): FastLED treats them as different chips. For WS2812 it uses 250/625/375 ns (T0H 250, T1H 875, T0L 1000, T1L 375). For WS2812B-V5 it uses 220/360/580 ns (T0H 220, T1H 580, T0L 940, T1L 580). Both come from the comments in `chipsets.h`. That supports C6b's thesis. I did not fetch Adafruit NeoPixel source; from memory it targets about 0.4 us / 0.8 us for the 800 kHz case, which I did not verify.

**The gap rule is out of spec on paper.** C6b says the inter-row gap "only adds to T_L" and must stay under 50 us. In practice only the reset threshold matters, so this works on real parts. But the nominal T0L max is 1.0 us and the old T1L max is 0.60 us, so a gap of 0.6 us plus an ISR is formally out of spec for the last bit of each row on both variants. Mark it "empirical, scope-verify", not "in spec". More important, the 30 us ISR budget is **per ISR**, and two or three ISRs can be pending at once (UART, INT0, timer). Two 30 us ISRs already reach 60 us, above the old part's 50 us reset. Budget the **sum** of ISR time in one gap (suggest 15 us per ISR, 40 us total), or measure it.

## 2. Interrupt masking facts

- DISI: `DISI #lit14`, lit14 in [0, 16383]. **DS70157 says it disables interrupts for (lit14 + 1) instruction cycles**, so the real maximum is 16384 cycles = 1.024 ms, not 16383 (nit). It disables priority 0 to 6 only; priority 7 and traps still run (DS70157 DISI note; DS39881E section 7.4). C6b's conclusion is right: a 30720-cycle frame cannot be covered, and `SRbits.IPL = 7` is the correct tool. C6b should add that IPL = 7 also blocks priority-7 user ISRs, which DISI would not.
- UART RX FIFO is 4 deep: DS39881E feature list ("4-level deep FIFO") and UART chapter ("4-Deep FIFO Receive Data Buffer"). **CONFIRMED.** URXISEL: 00/01 = interrupt at 1 char, 10 = 3 chars, 11 = 4 chars.
- Overflow timing at 115200 8N1: 86.8 us per char. OERR is set when the receive shift register completes a 5th character while 4 are unread. Worst case: one char is already waiting (ISR pending when masking starts), so 3 more fill the FIFO and the 4th overflows. That is **3 x 86.8 = 260 us minimum, up to 347 us**. C6b says "about 350 to 430 us", which is only the empty-FIFO case. Its per-row mask (240 us + gap) therefore has **almost no margin** against a continuously transmitting PC, and "2.9 chars is safe" is only true if every gap fully drains the FIFO. Rules to add: the UART RX ISR must loop `while (U1STAbits.URXDA)` (one char per interrupt is not enough), and the host must not transmit during a show, or the chunk must shrink to 4 LEDs (120 us).

**Is reserving 0xA000 to 0xA7FF safe?** Experiments (xc16-gcc v2.10, `-mcpu=24FJ64GA002`, linked with the real `.gld`):
- The reservation works with `__attribute__((space(prog), address(0xA000), aligned(2), noload)) const uint8_t rsv[2048];`. The linker emits a 0x800-byte absolute section at 0xA000 flagged NEVER_LOAD. `fillupper` is **not needed**, so C7's UNVERIFIED syntax note is resolved with a corrected form. 2048 B of array = 1024 instruction words, i.e. **2 bytes per word**.
- By default, the best-fit allocator places code and `.const` from the **bottom** (0x200 upward; my test `.const` load address was 0x2ce). With about 25 KB of flash use (about 0x4400 address units) nothing reaches 0xA000, so the reservation is safe in practice even without the attribute; only the attribute makes it a guarantee.
- Side effect observed: once 0xA000 to 0xA7FF is reserved, the best-fit allocator put a small `.text` section (main) at **0xA800**, inside block C. Block C is not empty and can hold code. "Never touched" must mean "never erased"; the erase routine must reject any address at or above 0xA800 (as C7 already says).
- A reservation at 0xA800 of 2048 B fails with "crosses the boundary of region program" and "Could not allocate program memory", so overlap with the config words is caught.
- **The device linker script is not applied automatically in my test.** Linking on the command line without `-Wl,-T,.../p24FJ64GA002.gld` used a generic memory map (program origin 0x100 length 0x3FF00, data length 0xF800), not the 8 KB / 64 KB limits. With `-T`, the report shows program 0x200 to 0xABFC and data 0x2000. Anyone measuring the budget (ticket 3.10) or running spike builds must pass the script, or use MPLAB X.
- PSV window: PSVPAG selects 16K-word pages (DS39881E section 4.3). 0xA000 to 0xA7FF is in page 1, while default `.const` sits in page 0. Reading the template blob via PSV needs PSVPAG = 1 (or `TBLRDL` with a `__prog__` pointer). C7 says only "reading through PSV".

**Persistence design bug (high).** C7 section 4.3 computes 2 x 512 x 2 B = 2048 B "usable" and notes one block gives 1024 B, less than 1200 B + header. It then adopts A/B alternation: write the new copy to the *other* block. Each copy must fit in one block, i.e. 1024 B at 2 B/word. **1216 B does not fit in 1024 B.** Either use all 3 bytes per word via `TBLRDH/TBLWTH` (1536 B per block, 1216 B fits, more code and a second read path), or use four blocks (6 KB reserved), or shrink the blob (for example 3 classes x 5 x 60 = 900 B + header). Update the flash budget accordingly (section 5).

## 6. Is the bulb recommendation sound?

Yes for a student demo: IR NEC primary, WS2812 ring as the guaranteed fallback. It matches the "color-changing light bulb" wording, uses one pin and one OC module, and keeps the PIC away from mains.

The 38 kHz carrier arithmetic checks (Timer2 period 421 cycles gives 38.005 kHz; 25% duty is OCxRS about 105). The PIC pin sources up to 18 mA (DS39881E), so an NPN driver is correct. NEC frame details match the public NEC description as I know it; I did not re-fetch it.

Risks and safety notes:
- **The unknown remote protocol is the main risk.** Buy the bulb or controller only after capturing its remote with an IR receiver, or choose one with a documented protocol. Keep option b (12 V strip) as the wired plan B.
- **Mains.** The wand never touches mains, but the bulb does. Use an enclosed commercial lamp with a normal plug, never exposed wiring, and get instructor sign-off. The relay option (c) is correctly rejected; only a ready-made enclosed module should even be considered.
- **12 V strip (option b):** fuse the 12 V supply, keep the strip short, use logic-level MOSFETs (gate drive at 3.3 V), common ground, and watch heat at full white.
- **WS2812 matrix:** the worst case at 5 V, 64 LEDs full white, is 3.84 A (Adafruit 60 mA per pixel). The proposed cap (32/255) plus a 300 mA software limiter is sensible, but the cap leaves only 33 levels per channel, so dim gradients will band. If power comes from USB through a serial bridge, 300 mA may exceed what the bridge requested; prefer a separate 5 V supply with a common ground. Keep the 500 to 1000 uF capacitor, the series resistor and the level shifter. A 74AHCT125 powered at 5 V accepts 3.3 V inputs and is the right choice.
- **VOH argument (D4).** C6b says the PIC's VOH is at least 3.0 V. The datasheet figure (DO20) is 3.0 V min at **VDD = 3.6 V, IOH = -3 mA**. At a 3.3 V supply the pin cannot exceed 3.3 V, so the old part's 3.5 V threshold (0.7 x 5 V) is not met and V5's 3.15 V (0.63 x 5 V) is marginal. The conclusion (use a level shifter) stands and is stronger than stated.
- **IR frames and WS2812 shows must not overlap.** C6b rule 4 is right. Long blocking work (flash writes, an 8.9 ms I2C burst at 100 kHz) delays the main loop but not the timer ISR, which is acceptable as long as that ISR stays short.
- RB12 to RB15 are not 5.5 V tolerant (DS39881E; C6a notes this). Never connect 5 V logic back to them.
